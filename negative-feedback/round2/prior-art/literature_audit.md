# Closest-prior-art audit: what the existing results actually imply

2026-10-22. Primary texts were opened and the specified statements/sections read; this does not mean that every proof in every paper was rederived. The audit distinguishes old sources reread for this claim from sources newly added in round2. Search covered the named foundational work and targeted 2024-2026 queries for adaptive sampling, local stability, negative count, feature suppression and false-negative filters. Lack of a search hit is not a proof of novelty.

The bottom-half rank counterexample is therefore evidence about an instantaneous quantile policy, not a reproduced failure of GloFND. Its alpha=.5, epsilon floor, finite atomic ties, identity positives and one-negative InfoNCE differ from that algorithm's threshold state, typical fraction and global negative-only objective. These differences are material to interpretation. Rank filtering's use in practice motivates investigating the boundary; it does not make the particular counterexample an empirical refutation of the method.

## Final independent recommendation

Reject the broad claim that “adaptive filtering can destabilize an otherwise good objective” is itself new. Retain the exact finite-K slope law as the main contribution candidate, with its complete proof and full-tangent controls. The newly executed DCL control rules out a pure NPC/learning-speed explanation; the natural exponential failure case forces all-mode scope discipline. An analytically constructed natural exponential follow-up subsequently succeeded for balance and normal_ab, so it would also be wrong to argue that all rescue is an artificial shaped-policy effect.

After the independent controls, the theory owner supplied and executed the full-spectrum DCL extension and an open natural exponential count-rescue theorem. This reviewer read theorem.md Sections8-9, checked the condition reduction `w/v>e` to `rho>(e−1)/(exp(t)−1)`, the nonempty interval proof `exp(t)−1>t(e−1)`, and the finite-cutoff logic. The scalar moments independently computed here agree with InfoNCE onsetK5 for epsilon=.3,t=log4; the owner's separate d4 full-field checks validate all modes and DCL onsetK8. These are direct mathematical contributions, not results borrowed from external papers.

**Final recommendation: proceed as a narrowly stated theoretical paper.** Its central result now exists: an exact full-spectrum/count law plus a proved open natural-policy region, rather than a still-unverified core hypothesis. This is a defensible restricted mathematical result with practical motivation, not a general theory of semantic information loss, realistic false-negative detection or all trained encoders. The remaining importance risk concerns paper scope and breadth, not the truth of the verified local claim. The paper's concrete contribution is a quantitative limit/prediction, not a new generic feedback principle or a new filtering algorithm.

## Freshness boundary

As of2026-10-22, the most recent primary theory text actually examined here is the official ICLR2026 SnaPhArd paper, including its stated collapse and negative-count results. The 2025 Jiang TMLR article, Apr2025 AnInfoNCE revision, and Jun2025 GloFND revision were checked in full-text form for the closest claims. Targeted2024-2026 search results were screened, but no assertion of exhaustive coverage of all2026 submissions is made. Primary texts unavailable in this environment were explicitly excluded from theorem-level negative claims. Therefore the defensible wording is “the closest inspected results do not provide this exact law,” not “no prior work has ever analyzed this.”

The official TMLR index also led to an October2026 SPRINT entry. Its accessible Sep2025 arXiv v2 assumptions and convergence theorem were read and added above. We do not infer that this is identical to every change in the final2026 TMLR version. In particular, the paper must not claim that existing performative theory requires strong convexity: nonconvex stationarity theory already exists, though stationarity does not resolve local attraction.

Jiang version-date ambiguity is preserved: the arXiv v3 page is timestamped7May2025, while the HTML manuscript body displays24August2026. The arXiv abstract page explicitly says acceptedApril2025 and journal-reference TMLR2025; the official TMLR accepted-papers index independently lists the paper underMay2025. OpenReview's forum/PDF endpoints presented a verification challenge in this environment, so their final rendered PDF was not independently retrieved here. We therefore cite the inspected arXiv version for statement/proof scope and the official index for venue/date, without inferring that the HTML body date is a verified new2026 revision.
