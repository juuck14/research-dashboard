# Closest primary results and inherited knowledge

2026-10-23. This audit does not infer novelty from different notation or a missing identical title. It checks which pieces of this branch are already supplied by known theory and which remain to be judged with the combined dynamical failure.

## Awasthi, Dikkala, Kamath, ICML2022

*Do More Negative Samples Necessarily Hurt In Contrastive Learning?* [Published primary PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/awasthi22b/awasthi22b.pdf). Read §2–3 assumptions, Theorem3.8 and Claim3.9, the proof sketch and AppendixA.2's Jensen/Gram argument, plus §3.3's count conclusion.

Their fixed class-law NCE population optimizer under nonoverlapping uniform latent classes is a simplex ETF, independent of the negative count. Strict convex losses have only that Gram optimum up to null-set representation differences. Their proof uses Jensen symmetrization and the lower bound−1/(C−1) on the mean off-class inner product. The frozen global-optimum proof here is the same established structural reasoning, adapted to the explicit uniform-off law or the simplex's fixed self/off law. It cannot be called a new optimization principle. Their theorem does not cover an encoder-dependent detached policy response or bad stable equilibria of that different vector field; our result is not a counterexample to their fixed-law theorem.

## Graf, Hofer, Niethammer, Kwitt, ICML2021

*Dissecting Supervised Contrastive Learning*. [Published primary PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/graf21a/graf21a.pdf). Read §3.1–3.2, Theorem2 and its proof idea, and §4's relation to neural collapse. The journal-independent learning target of separated equal-class simplex features is not invented by the present project. Their theorem identifies the loss-minimizing geometry of supervised contrastive learning under balanced labels and sphere constraints; it does not analyze the present detached adaptive sampling. The presence of a different loss or policy alone does not make our local coefficient a top-level contribution.

## Tang, Tan, Han, September2026 preprint

*Sharp Rates and a One-Line Correction for Spectral Representation Learning*, [primary v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15825v1). Read §2's task/covariance setup, Proposition4, §3 Theorem6 and its sharpness argument, and §5's objective correction. Their downstream usefulness depends on a task covariance rather than a task-free feature spectrum; they analyze alignment with the spectral operator and quantify task-prior dependence. It is an unpublished inspected preprint, not used as a replacement or lowering of the fixed JMLR comparator. It reinforces that isotropic/task-weighted spectral utility is established territory. It does not provide the finite-K simplex feedback instability or the proposed all-count coarse attraction theorem.

## Mathematical ingredients not counted as novel

Population affine risk is the standard linear least-squares/LMMSE covariance identity. The simplex optimum follows from strict concavity under a fixed trace, and the shape decomposition uses ordinary simplex/permutation geometry. The hard part of a possible paper cannot be merely obtaining these familiar facts. The proposed new knowledge must be the complete separation: a globally and task-independently justified target, the same full-support correct-negative law at that target, sharp count failure of the updating field, and an actually bad attracting limit. Generic performative-Jacobian arguments already explain that feedback can change stability; whether the specific complete failure meets the fixed journal bar requires the separate adversarial audit.
